Two power domains, joined only by ground

The single decision that shapes everything else: the Pi runs on its own supply, off unswitched mains. The e‑stop drops the machine and leaves the computer alive.

The Octopus can power a Pi — it has a dedicated header (J26) and an 8 A 5 V buck, so this isn't a capacity problem. It's a behaviour problem. Hang the Pi off that rail and every e‑stop, every power-down and every fault kills the computer mid-cut: lost machine position, no controlled recovery, and an unclean NVMe shutdown every single time.

Wiring consequence

With two supplies, connect ground and the SPI signals only. Do not bridge 5 V between the Pi and the Octopus — two supplies fighting over one rail is how you cook a regulator. Bond the grounds properly; SPI needs a solid reference, not an incidental one.

Secondary, but real: a Pi 5 can't negotiate USB‑PD through the GPIO header, so it boots into reduced-power mode and caps total USB current at 600 mA — awkward when the NVMe HAT is already drawing its own 2–5 W.

Signal chain

Step generation happens on the Octopus, not the Pi. The Pi runs the 1 kHz servo thread and one SPI transfer per cycle — which is why a Pi is the right host here and an x86 box isn't.

EMI — this one bites late

Keep the SPI ribbon physically away from the spindle motor cable, and keep it short. VFD switching noise coupling into SPI is a leading cause of Remora comms errors, and it presents as a software problem — random link drops, e‑stops mid-program — which is exactly the wrong place to go looking. Use shielded cable for the spindle motor run and bond the shield at the VFD end only; grounding both ends invites a loop.

Verify before you land these

Those designations come from the Milo documentation, which assumes a Mitsubishi‑style terminal layout. VFD terminal numbering varies by manufacturer. On many drives one of the low-numbered analog terminals is a 10 V reference output, not an input — wiring your 0–10 V signal into it shorts an output against a source. Check your drive's own manual and confirm which terminal is the analog input before connecting anything.

E-stop chain

The e‑stop breaks the contactor coil circuit directly, in copper. LinuxCNC finding out about it is awareness, not the safety function — never make software the thing that stops the machine.

  • Hardware path: latching mushroom button, normally-closed, in series with the contactor coil. Press it and the coil drops out, taking the 24 V PSU and the VFD's mains with it. Release-and-press-start to restore, so power never comes back on its own.
  • Software path: an auxiliary NC contact on the contactor into an Octopus input, so LinuxCNC knows the machine is dead and refuses to run. This is for state, not for stopping.
  • Free watchdog: Remora's SPI-status pin already drops LinuxCNC's enable if the link to the Octopus fails. It's wired in milo.hal — keep it.
Worth designing for now

Killing mains to a VFD while the spindle is turning is hard on the drive, and the spindle then coasts for a long time with a tool in the work. The better arrangement uses the VFD's own safe-stop or external-fault input to brake the spindle, with the contactor as the backstop behind it. Decide this before you build the panel — it's a wiring change, not a settings change.

Before you cut a wire

Seven things this drawing can't settle on its own — two now answered. Each of the rest has a definite answer sitting on the hardware or in a manual.

02Which output drives the expansion board's PWM in OpenOn the Milo's original mainboard this was the BLTouch connector. The Octopus equivalent depends on what Remora exposes as a PWM channel — also in config.json.
03Which output drives the enable relay OpenNeeds to be an output Remora holds in a defined state at power-up. A spindle that starts on boot is the failure you don't get a second look at.
04Endstop input numbers, and switch polarity OpenThree switches: X at min, Y at max, Z at max. Verify each in halshow with motors unpowered before homing anything.
05Which driver modules are in the Octopus OpenRepRapFirmware asked for 1800 mA on X and Y, which is at the ceiling of a TMC2209's usable range and a non-issue on a TMC5160. Read the labels on the modules.
